01 / Overview

Project overview.

CardPG is a single-player card roguelike prototype. Players use cards to attack, discard cards to block incoming damage, and manage persistent HP. The playable prototype expands the core combat loop with branching routes, events, shops, upgrades, boss encounters, and run-level build decisions.

A run begins with 40 cards - Ace through Ten in four suits - and progresses through 12 maps. Defeating the four Jacks, four Queens, and four Kings adds the exact defeated boss card to the player's collection.

02 / Combat design

Combat systems.

Normal combat commits one card against a selected enemy. During defense, cards are assigned to distinct pending enemy attacks and must fully cover each assigned attack; excess block is not transferred. The player can take the remaining damage. Recover is available with an empty hand at the cost of one incoming enemy attack. Enemies remain independently targetable.

Baseline combat

Card use creates a trade-off between immediate attack value, future hand resources, and the ability to block.

Build-dependent options

Ace pairing combines an Ace with one other card into a single seeded-critical attack. Other multi-card play patterns come from specific Artifacts rather than being universal rules.

Artifacts affect a run's rules and modifiers; Enhancements belong to specific card instances. Consumables add tactical decisions such as card duplication, destruction, and suit changes.

Feature deep dive

Ace Pairing

Exactly two cards, with at least one Ace, can be committed as one attack. Per-card damage combines before a single action-level critical roll.

03 / Run design

Run progression.

Generated routes combine Combat, Elite, Shop, Event, Upgrade, Risk, and Boss nodes. Shops and upgrades affect the current build while bosses award exact face cards that persist for the remainder of the run.

Independent deterministic random streams allow replaying a run seed without coupling card draws, shops, and map generation into one mutable stream. The standard 12-map run ends after the final King; Infinite Mode exists as an opt-in prototype continuation but is still awaiting owner phase acceptance.

04 / Technical implementation

Gameplay architecture.

Shared ScriptableObject assets hold authorable definitions while runtime instances carry mutable per-run data. Cards use stable run-local identity, with authoritative ownership and deck, hand, and discard zones. The Unity UI presents and manipulates those models without becoming the source of truth.

Shared effect architecture

Typed triggers, modifiers, and resolvers provide a foundation for Artifact and Enhancement interactions, limiting reliance on hard-coded item names and improving extension and testing.

05 / Design decisions

Technical challenges.

Effect complexity

Problem: Interacting card effects and upgrades become difficult to maintain as content grows.

Approach: A typed effect pipeline with definitions separate from card- and run-owned state.

UI and card identity

Problem: Hand dragging and sorting must not change authoritative gameplay state.

Approach: Keep visual card order separate from stable card identities and model-owned collections.

Gameplay feedback, mouse/drag feel, and final balance remain under iteration. The project has not completed a recorded twelve-map human pacing study. Some recent expansion features, including Artifact tiers, Contracts, Blood Ritual, and Infinite Mode, are implemented in prototype form but await phase acceptance; workbook-only draft abilities are not presented as live mechanics.

06 / Verification

Testing & validation.

CardPG uses Unity EditMode and PlayMode tests to cover combat resolution, deterministic runs, progression, inventory behavior, and gameplay interactions. The repository documents a historical passing checkpoint of 329 EditMode tests and 3 PlayMode tests; those tests were not rerun for this portfolio.

Project status: Playable prototype, actively developed. No public standalone release or save/resume flow is documented.
