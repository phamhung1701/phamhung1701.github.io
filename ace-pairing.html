Ace Pairing.

A two-card attack rule for CardPG. This feature case study covers its objective, eligibility, combat resolution, deterministic criticals, and verification boundaries.

Feature
Ace Pairing
Rule
Exactly two cards, at least one Ace

01 / Design objective

Give Aces a distinct role.

Normal CardPG combat uses one card per attack. An Ace has base attack value 1; Ace Pairing allows a low-value card to enable a more powerful combined action, without introducing unrestricted poker-hand play.

The cost is immediate: using a pair commits two cards at once. Neither card remains available for defense, and both attacks target a single selected enemy.

Legal action

Ace + Eight

Two cards are committed together and their independently modified damage is combined. The action uses one critical roll, not two.

02 / Rule boundaries

Explicit eligibility.

SelectionResult
Ace + EightValid Ace Pair
Ace + AceValid Ace Pair; one action and one critical roll
Two + EightRejected; no cards consumed, no critical RNG used
Single cardNormal single-card action
Three cardsNot a valid Ace Pair; separate Artifact rules may enable other groups

Card validation occurs before commitment. Invalid pairs cannot consume cards or change the seeded critical stream. Royal Family and same-rank Hands are separate Artifact-gated rule families.

03 / Technical implementation

One combat action.

01Validate two-card selection and exact enemy target
02Atomically commit both cards
03Calculate each card's modified attack and sum
04Apply action-level rules and one eligible seeded critical roll
05Resolve damage, ordered effects and conditional Ace return

Applicable on-play effects execute once per card in selection order. Effects tied to the whole action are not doubled merely because two cards were used. A successful critical doubles the combined action damage once, after any applicable action cap. On a critical Ace Pair, returning an Ace to hand is attempted subject to capacity and the runtime return rule.

Critical RNG uses a dedicated seeded combat stream, separate from map, card and shop random streams. Damage targeting stays on the originally selected enemy; later hits do not automatically retarget when it dies.

04 / Gameplay trade-offs

Burst damage vs. hand resources.

Offensive opportunity

Pairing can remove a dangerous enemy before it attacks. Critical damage creates burst potential, and effects on both cards can influence the final result.

Defensive opportunity cost

Two cards leave the hand together. With a limited hand and no automatic per-turn refill, those cards can no longer cover separate enemy attacks.

Even with a 25% default critical chance, the feature is not known to be numerically balanced. Artifact overrides, damage caps, hand capacity, and the conditional Ace return change its value in a given run.

05 / Evidence and limits

Verified behaviors.

Focused Unity EditMode tests cover pair legality, damage and card consumption, Ace + Ace, invalid non-Ace pairs, RNG consumption boundaries, guaranteed critical cases, and on-play effect order. A recorded Game-scene integration check used Unity EventSystem-dispatched selection and action-button flows.

Verification scope: The documented Game-scene check was not a human mouse/drag usability session. The 25% default chance and feature balance remain prototype tuning; no validated human balancing result is claimed.

Primary inspected files cited in the source notes include CombatManager.cs, GameplayEffects.cs, CardData.cs, and Phase7CCombatPairTests.cs. The October 9 dirty UI/card-view worktree changes were not verified by this case study.

06 / Future iteration

What to evaluate next.

  • Record real playthrough decisions: when players choose a pair, hold it for later, or reserve cards for defense.
  • Improve selection cues, paired damage preview, and explanation of critical chance and the conditional Ace return.
  • Compare different critical probabilities only after playtests identify a specific balance issue.
